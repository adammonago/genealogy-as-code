# Identity Bridge Resolution

How to work a same-name collision, alias-heavy branch, spouse split, or household-structure problem without over-merging people too early.

## When to Use This Workflow

Use this when the main problem is not "find any source" but:

- deciding whether two records describe the same person
- separating multiple people with the same or similar names
- sorting one person across multiple surname forms or language variants
- evaluating whether one apparent spouse cluster is really two households
- preserving a working model while the decisive bridge record is still missing

## Step 1: State the Identity Problem

Write the question in one sentence.

Examples:

- Is `Joel Levi` the same man as `Joseph / Josel Levi`, or are they related but distinct?
- Do these two spouse clusters belong to one `Gabriel Cohen` or to two different men?

## Step 2: Build the Candidate Table

Create a table with one row per candidate identity:

| Candidate | Supporting records | Conflicting records | Shared anchors | Keep separate because | Next bridge record |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Candidate label] | [sources] | [sources] | [town, house number, parent, spouse, occupation] | [risk of over-merge] | [record type most likely to decide] |

## Step 3: Preserve Every Anchor

For each candidate, preserve:

- full name variants
- patronymics or Hebrew/Yiddish forms
- spouses
- parents
- children
- house numbers or street addresses
- occupations
- witnesses, informants, or allied-family names
- towns, districts, and migration path

Treat house numbers and addresses as evidence-bearing household anchors when available.

## Step 4: Score the Bridge Strength

For every proposed merge, ask:

1. Do the records share a rare anchor, or only a common name?
2. Are parent names stable, or drifting?
3. Are spouse names stable, or suggesting two households?
4. Is the chronology realistic?
5. Is there any record that explicitly bridges the two forms?

## Step 5: Resolve, Split, or Hold

### Resolve

Merge only when the bridge is strong enough to meet the source hierarchy and confidence rules.

### Split

If the records clearly represent different people, keep them separate and note the distinguishing anchors.

### Hold

If the evidence is suggestive but incomplete:

- keep a working model
- mark the uncertain links clearly
- do not collapse the branch into one identity yet

## Step 6: Name the Decisive Next Record

Do not stop at "needs more research."

Identify the exact best next record type, such as:

- child birth record naming both parents
- marriage record linking parent pair and place
- passenger manifest with nearest relative
- naturalization record with exact birthplace
- death record naming parents
- household list or tax record tied to a house number

## Step 7: Propagate Conservatively

When updating the vault:

- preserve alternate names in aliases or notes
- keep speculative household models labeled as hypotheses
- separate evidence from interpretation
- update Open_Questions.md if the identity problem remains open

## Required Output Checklist

Every run of this workflow should explicitly include:

1. The identity question
2. Candidate table
3. Strongest anchors
4. Merge / split / hold decision
5. Exact next bridge record
